# Long-range quantum criticality, high-order series for long-range spin models, and real long-range magnets: where GZL can produce new results

Scope note: research done 2026-09-25 via web search + arXiv abstract pages. All arXiv IDs below were seen on arxiv.org (abs/html/pdf) or in search results pointing to arxiv.org in this session unless marked "UNVERIFIED". Abstract contents were read via a summarizing fetch tool, so fine details (exact exponents, parameter values) were NOT extracted and are listed as gaps. ## Q1. State of the art 2025–2026 on long-range quantum criticality; disputed / poorly known quantities

### Takeaway
The central live dispute is the location of the long-range(LR) to short-range(SR) crossover: Sak's criterion (sigma* = 2 - eta_SR) versus a universal sigma* = 2, now claimed for classical 2D Ising/XY/Heisenberg by large-scale MC (Deng/Fan group, 2025–2026) and for quantum O(n) models by a 2026 perturbative RG paper; high-precision quantum-lattice data that settle it for the LRTFIM and LR XXZ/Heisenberg models do not exist yet. The second weak spot is the "continuously varying exponents" window (between LR mean-field and SR universality), where pCUT+MC and QMC give exponents with sizeable error bars and where log corrections at sigma = 2d/3 (quantum upper critical boundary) complicate extrapolation.

### Cited Findings
- GZL physics paper: Duft, Adelhardt, Koziol, Buchheit, Schmidt, "Exact and fast series expansions for quantum models with long-range interactions", submitted 2026-09-16. Replaces MC evaluation of embedding sums by graph zeta functions (Epstein zeta for low-treewidth blocks, tensor-network bucket elimination for higher treewidth); benchmarks LRTFIM on chain, square, triangular, cubic lattices; 0qp ground-state energy and 1qp dispersion over the full BZ; dense scan of the decay exponent "yielding a detailed characterization of the quantum phase diagram and the crossover between universality regimes"; GZL coefficients agree with MC series within MC error and are the "more accurate reference" — [arXiv:2609.18761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18761), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18761)
- Stated outlook of 2609.18761 (as extracted): extension to Heisenberg/XY/XXZ ("straightforward generalization"), multi-site unit cells, higher quasiparticle sectors, bound states, finite-temperature properties, further materials; kernel must be regularised at zero and absolutely summable; Bravais lattices only — [arXiv:2609.18761 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18761)

### Inferences
- The most "settle-able" disputed quantity for GZL is the quantum Sak boundary for the LRTFIM (and O(n) analogues): sigma* = 2 - eta_SR(d+1) (Sak-type: 7/4 for d = 1 since the SR class is 2D Ising; 2 - eta_3DIsing ≈ 1.96 for d = 2) versus sigma* = 2 (2606.22407). In d = 2 the two candidates differ by only ~0.04, which is below the resolution of MC-noisy series; deterministic coefficients plus a dense sigma grid (cheap with GZL) are exactly what is needed to test e.g. whether the gap exponent z·nu reaches its SR value at 1.75 or only at 2 in d = 1. Caveat: series of order 11–13 with DlogPadé extrapolation may still be limited by the order and by slow crossover (log corrections near the boundary); a precise statement is "GZL removes the coefficient noise; the order-limited extrapolation error remains". This is an inference, not verified in 2609.18761 (the fetch did not return crossover values).
- 2609.18761 already does a "high-resolution scan of the decay exponent" for the LRTFIM, so a pure LRTFIM Sak study partially overlaps with the authors' own paper; novelty would have to come from (a) explicit confrontation with the 2026 sigma* = 2 RG prediction and the Deng-group MC claims, (b) the d = 2 triangular/square AF and 3D cubic cases, (c) extending to O(2)/O(3) (XXZ/Heisenberg) where the 2606.22407 predictions are made for general n.
- Log corrections at the quantum upper critical boundary sigma_uc = 2d/3 (and in d = 3 the SR class is 4D Ising with logs for the whole SR regime) are a natural precision target: multiplicative logs in the gap Delta ~ |lambda - lambda_c|^{1/2} |ln|^{p} are notoriously hard to see with noisy coefficients; with exact coefficients one can fit ratio-method forms with log terms. The 3D cubic LRTFIM (supported by GZL) is a clean candidate.

## Q2. Methods and their lattice-sum bottlenecks

### Takeaway
pCUT + classical-MC embedding (Schmidt group) is the only high-order thermodynamic-limit series method in active use for LR quantum magnets; its bottleneck is exactly the MC evaluation of high-dimensional power-law embedding sums (statistical noise, cost per momentum, slow convergence near sigma → 0 / nu → d). QMC (SSE), MPS/DMRG, NQS and RG/bootstrap are used in parallel and suffer from finite-size / boundary-condition effects (explicitly reported for LR chains) rather than from lattice sums.

### Cited Findings
- Review of MC-based techniques: pCUT with classical MC integration and SSE QMC for 1D/2D quantum magnets with LR Ising, XY and Heisenberg interactions on bipartite and non-bipartite lattices, critical exponents incl. above the upper critical dimension (Adelhardt, Koziol, Langheld, Schmidt, 2024-03-01) — [arXiv:2403.00421](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.00421)
- 2609.18761 explicitly frames MC evaluation of embedding sums as "costly and statistically noisy" and the graph zeta method as delivering coefficients "within minutes on standard desktop hardware", with speedups of "several orders of magnitude" — [arXiv:2609.18761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18761)
- pCUT+MC still used in 2026 for new LR problems (spin-1 chain with staggered LR interactions) in combination with MPS; boundary conditions significantly influence finite-size scaling in MPS for long-range potentials — [arXiv:2604.12754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12754)
- pCUT+MC with white graphs for LR Heisenberg ladders (energy, one-triplon gap, spectral weight) — [arXiv:2209.01182](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.01182); pCUT+MC for LR XXZ bilayer (dispersion and spectral weight) — [arXiv:2408.13145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13145)
- Classical MC with L up to 8192 for 2D LR Ising/Heisenberg (Deng group) — [arXiv:2512.04805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04805), [arXiv:2512.01956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01956)
- RG with controlled epsilon expansion for LR quantum O(n) — [arXiv:2606.22407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22407); 1D LR bootstrap sum rules — [arXiv:2603.22395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22395)
- NQS/VMC for AF LR Ising chain — [arXiv:2308.09709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09709); MPS for LR anisotropic Heisenberg chain DQCP — [arXiv:2311.06350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06350)
- Spin-wave-type / Holstein–Primakoff and mean-field theory used to interpret a 2026 trapped-ion 2D LR XY experiment on Gaussian vs non-Gaussian magnon dynamics — [arXiv:2606.13499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13499)
- McPhase simulations used to model dipolar + Heisenberg competition in NaGdS2 — [arXiv:2512.10714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10714)

### Inferences
- Where lattice sums are the bottleneck: (i) pCUT+MC in all its uses (the prime target); (ii) spin-wave / RPA / McPhase-type modelling of dipolar magnets — but there the sums are single-edge Fourier transforms J(k) (Ewald-type), which are already cheap; GZL adds value only when products of couplings (higher-order spin-wave / 1/S corrections, or series) are needed; (iii) classical high-temperature series for LR models (not found in current literature — see Q4) would need exactly graph zeta sums.
- QMC, MPS, NQS and bootstrap do not have a lattice-sum bottleneck; GZL's contribution there is as an independent, thermodynamic-limit cross-check (no finite-size/boundary artifacts, which 2604.12754 identifies as a problem for LR chains).
- LR Kitaev chains are free-fermion problems: all needed lattice sums are one-edge polylog-type Fourier transforms; GZL gives essentially nothing new there (low priority). Interacting LR Kitaev/XY variants would be a different matter but need own corpora.

### Gaps
- No source found quantifying the MC error bars of pCUT+MC coefficients vs. order (would make the GZL gain quantitative for each earlier paper); the Zenodo raw data of 2408.13145 might contain them — not checked.
- No evidence found of other groups (outside Erlangen) using linked-cluster/pCUT for LR models in 2025–2026.

## Q3. Real materials 2025–2026 with dipolar / long-range couplings; neutron data for full-BZ comparison

### Takeaway
The best-matched materials are rare-earth triangular-lattice Ising magnets with non-Kramers Tm3+ (KTmSe2, NaTmSe2, TmMgGaO4) and related chalcogenides (KErSe2), because they are single-site Bravais (triangular, ABC-stacked) with an intrinsic transverse field and dispersive crystal-field/"quasi-doublet" excitations measured by inelastic neutron scattering; with moments along c, the in-plane dipolar zz tail is isotropic 1/r^3, which GZL supports today. LiHoF4, pyrochlore spin ice, NaGdS2 (full Heisenberg + dipolar tensor) need anisotropic dipolar tails and/or multi-site lattices (roadmap items).

### Cited Findings
- KTmSe2: triangular sheets of non-Kramers Tm3+ with ABC stacking; no LRO from heat capacity and neutron diffraction; strong Ising AF correlations; INS shows a dispersive CEF branch from 0.85 to 1.6 meV (first excited CEF state), analysed with TFIM + full CEF + exchange (Zhao group, PRB 108, 054435 (2023)) — [arXiv:2306.03544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03544), [PRB](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.108.054435), [PDF Fudan](http://jzhao.fudan.edu.cn/pdfs/Exchange-renormalized%20crystal%20field%20excitations%20in%20the%20quantum%20Ising%20magnet%20KTmSe2.pdf)
- 2609.18761 compares microscopic models for KTmSe2 (2D and 3D variants) and finds "a nearest-neighbor model supplemented by the full long-range dipolar interaction gives a very accurate description of the measured dispersion" (INS) — [arXiv:2609.18761](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18761)
- NaTmSe2 (Zheng Zhang et al., 2025-05-15): realizes the TFIM with quantitatively determined exchange parameters; multipolar spin-polarized state coexisting with a dipolar spin-disordered state; gapless spinon excitations mediated by multipolar moments; magnetization, specific heat, neutron scattering, muSR — [arXiv:2505.09884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09884)
- TmMgGaO4: Sz dipolar component forms a 3-sublattice structure; transverse multipolar components show ferro-multipolar order visible in INS (Nat. Commun. 2019, anchor) — [Nature Commun.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12410-3)
- KErSe2: stripe LRO below 0.2 K, spin-wave dispersion with ~0.03 meV gap at M, negligible z-modulation (from search-result summary; primary paper not opened) — [search result pointing to TmMgGaO4/KErSe2 literature, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354124540_Field-Tuned_Quantum_Effects_in_a_Triangular-Lattice_Ising_Magnet) (UNVERIFIED attribution — the KErSe2 statement came from a search snippet, primary arXiv ID not identified)
- SrTm2O4: pseudo-doublet ground state of a non-Kramers compound with frustrated AF interactions (2025) — [arXiv:2501.12035](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12035v3)
- NaGdS2 (Grumbach, Häußler, Luther et al., 2025-12-11): large Gd3+ moment ⇒ strong dipolar coupling; "magnetic order is suppressed by a competition between Heisenberg and dipolar interactions"; modelled with McPhase — [arXiv:2512.10714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10714)
- LiHoF4: "spin-half non-standard quantum Ising system" — off-diagonal dipolar terms derived perturbatively in the low-energy effective Hamiltonian with and without transverse field — [arXiv:2308.10095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10095); soft-mode measurement at the QPT (microwave spectroscopy, electronuclear modes from Ho nuclear spin-7/2) — [arXiv:2101.05143](https://arxiv.org/pdf/arXiv:2101.05143v1); "A Gallery of Soft Modes: Theory and Experiment at a Ferromagnetic Quantum Phase Transition" (2024) — [arXiv:2408.03510](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03510v1); diluted LiHoxY1-xF4 phase diagram — [arXiv:1611.09777](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.09777) (anchors; no 2025–2026 LiHoF4 paper found in this session)
- LiErF4 dipolar antiferromagnet classical and quantum critical phenomena (EPFL) — [Infoscience](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/entities/publication/690ede7a-f900-4942-9c90-f7c91f65f8b4)
- Trapped-ion 2D XY with long-range, spatially structured interactions: crossover between Gaussian and non-Gaussian magnon dynamics (Guo, Tan, Ye et al., 2026-06-11) — [arXiv:2606.13499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13499)

### Inferences
- Moments along c + in-plane lattice: for Ising moments perpendicular to a 2D triangular plane, the dipolar zz coupling is D(1-3cos^2 theta)/r^3 with theta = 90°, i.e. exactly D/r^3 — isotropic, GZL-native (kernel = a(x) [J1, J2 compact] + D|x|^-3). Interlayer (3D) dipolar couplings carry angular dependence and are not an isotropic power law. How 2609.18761 treated the 3D stacked dipolar variant must be checked in the paper (GAP); if it used an approximation, the anisotropic-tail extension would give the first fully 3D dipolar series for KTmSe2.
- Direct next materials with a GZL-native model: NaTmSe2 (TFIM confirmed, parameters quantitatively determined, neutron data exist) and, in its disordered/field-polarized regime, TmMgGaO4. For NaTmSe2 the pCUT expansion around the transverse-field limit is valid only in the paramagnetic ("dipolar spin-disordered") regime — which is exactly the state reported. New result: first dipolar-inclusive, full-BZ 1qp dispersion and gap for NaTmSe2 and a model-selection test (J1-J2 vs J1 + dipolar) as done for KTmSe2; plus a quantitative estimate of the distance to the quantum critical point (gap-closing at K) with and without dipolar tail.
- LiHoF4 / LiErF4: Ho/Er sublattice is not a single-site Bravais lattice in the conventional cell (scheelite; primitive cell with 2 rare-earth ions per cell — from background knowledge, UNVERIFIED here), the dipolar tail is anisotropic (moments along c in 3D), and hyperfine coupling matters. Needs multi-site + anisotropic tail + effective-model derivation — a long-term target, not a first project.
- Pyrochlore spin ice (non-Bravais, 4-site basis, local <111> axes) — outside GZL's current scope; also anisotropic tail. Note as limitation.
- NaGdS2 is essentially classical (S = 7/2) Heisenberg + full dipolar tensor: spin-wave/1/S or classical high-T series would need anisotropic tails and vector-spin corpora — roadmap.
- Molecular-magnet crystals (e.g. Mn12-ac, Fe8) are single-molecule-per-cell dipolar Ising ferromagnets in transverse field on Bravais lattices (background knowledge, UNVERIFIED in this session) — conceptually ideal for GZL once anisotropic dipolar tails are supported.

## Q4. Further observables GZL-based series could provide

### Takeaway
Beyond 0qp energy and 1qp dispersion (shipped), the highest-value additions are (i) 1qp spectral weights (dynamic structure factor residues, giving the exponent eta/(2-eta)-type combination that is currently the least precise in pCUT+MC), (ii) 2qp bound states in the LRTFIM/XXZ, and (iii) classical high-temperature series for LR Ising/XY/Heisenberg to attack the classical Sak dispute independently of MC. All require new corpora (and for 2qp, multi-momentum graph sums), which is honest extra work.

### Cited Findings
- pCUT+MC already computes one-triplon spectral weights in LR ladders and the LR XXZ bilayer, used to extract exponents — [arXiv:2209.01182](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.01182), [arXiv:2408.13145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13145)
- 2609.18761 lists higher quasiparticle sectors, bound states and finite temperature as future directions — [arXiv:2609.18761](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18761)
- GZL README: shipped corpora are LRTFIM 0qp (order 13) and 1qp (order 11); "additional default models, such as the Heisenberg model, will be included as time progresses"; kernels are finite sums of regularised power laws plus a real, even, compactly supported short-range part — [GitHub graph-zeta/gzl](https://github.com/graph-zeta/gzl)
- Classical 2D LR Ising/XY/Heisenberg Sak dispute currently decided by MC only — [arXiv:2512.04805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04805), [arXiv:2411.01811](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.01811), [arXiv:2512.01956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01956)

### Inferences
- Spectral weight: the pCUT observable series have the same embedding-sum structure as energies (sum over graph placements of products of couplings), so they are GZL-evaluable given an observable corpus. New result: deterministic 1qp spectral-weight exponents for the LRTFIM on square/triangular/cubic lattices — and for KTmSe2 a prediction of the momentum-dependent neutron intensity, not only the dispersion (a sharper model-selection test than dispersion alone).
- Gap closing / critical points: already in 2609.18761. Added value mainly in new models/materials.
- 2qp bound states: require sums with two independent momenta / relative coordinates; GZL provides one external momentum per evaluation (per the brief). Feasibility depends on whether relative-coordinate amplitudes can be written as graph zeta functions with pinned vertices — needs method work (UNVERIFIED feasibility).
- Classical high-T series (LR Ising etc.): the free-embedding high-T expansion is a sum over placements of graph vertices with product of J(r) per edge — directly a graph zeta function. However, linked-cluster high-T expansions require handling of coincident vertices / excluded-volume corrections; whether GZL's "regularised at zero" kernel convention reproduces the needed free-vs-strong embedding bookkeeping must be checked in 2609.18918. If it works: an MC-independent estimate of sigma* in 2D LR Ising (7/4 vs 2) — a genuinely new data point in a live 2025–2026 dispute.
- Dynamic structure factor beyond 1qp (continua) is out of reach of current corpora.

## Q5. Candidate list: what exactly is new, and what must be built

### Takeaway
